Gentleman’s Clock

A game clock for any number of players, on one phone or many.

How it works

Set the time per turn and the time per game. Each player’s turn time starts fresh every turn; their game time never resets, and it only starts running once the turn time is used up.

To share a game with other phones, open the menu and choose Make public. Others join with the link or the five-letter code on this screen.

Choosing times

Time per turn suits games with long turns of several actions. Time per game suits games with short turns where most of the time goes to thinking, like chess. A mix of the two stops a player from running down the clock with fast, reckless moves.

Chess tournaments often give 5 seconds per turn on top of 45 minutes or more per game. Faster chess might use 10 minutes per game and 2 seconds per turn.

The name

Gentleman’s Clock is in the same vein as a gentleman’s bet: an arrangement based on the trust of everyone involved rather than anything binding. Once, while I took too long in a game, a friend said “Tick… tock… gentleman’s clock.” I thought it was funny, so that became the name.
